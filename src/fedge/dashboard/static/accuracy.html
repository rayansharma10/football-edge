What the numbers mean

Winner hit rate – how often the most likely outcome (home win, draw or away win) was right. Football is noisy: even strong models land around 50–55%.

Exact-score hit rate – how often the predicted scoreline was exactly right. This is inherently hard: the single most likely score only has a ~10–12% chance, so expect roughly 1 in 9 at best. The predicted score is always the most likely score within the predicted winner, so score and winner agree.

Top-3 / top-5 – the actual score was among the model’s 3 or 5 most likely scorelines. Goal difference – right margin (e.g. 2-1 predicted, 3-2 happened).

Brier score – average squared error of the W/D/L probabilities (lower is better; always guessing 33/33/33 scores about 0.667). Log-loss – how surprised the model was by what happened (lower is better; 1.099 = a 33/33/33 guess). RPS – like Brier but respects the order home<draw<away (lower is better).

Calibration – are the percentages honest? MAE goals – average gap between expected goals and goals scored. Score probability – the average log of the chance the model gave the real scoreline (higher, i.e. closer to 0, is better). Over 2.5 / BTTS – accuracy and Brier of the goal-market probabilities.

Baselines – always-home, league-average results, league-average scoreline and a naive Poisson from last season’s goal rates, scored on the same matches. A useful model should beat them.

d7 / d3 / final – the first prediction made at least 7 days / 3 days before kick-off, and the last one before kick-off. “Hours before” shows how early each really was. 95% range is a bootstrap interval; with fewer than 100 matches treat every number as a rough guide.

Predictions are logged by scripts/predict_upcoming.py, results by scripts/update_results.py. This page is read-only.
